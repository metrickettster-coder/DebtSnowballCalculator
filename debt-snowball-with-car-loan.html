Using the Debt Snowball With a Car Loan

Where a car loan fits in your payoff order, what changes when it's almost paid off, and the one payment you should never skip.

By M.T., PayoffSnowball editor · Published October 7, 2026 · Updated October 7, 2026 · How we research

Yes, list your car loan in your snowball, but expect it to come last. A car loan is usually your biggest balance and one of your lowest rates, so both the snowball (smallest balance first) and the avalanche (highest rate first) put it at the end. The exception is a car loan that's nearly paid off. Then the snowball may knock it out first, and its large monthly payment rolls into everything else.

Rule one: always pay the car loan on time

The snowball method only decides where your extra money goes. Every debt still gets its minimum payment every month, and the car loan matters most. It's a secured loan: the car is the collateral. The Federal Trade Commission notes that in many states a lender can repossess the car once you default, and a late payment can count as a default. Losing the car can also mean losing the way you get to work, which makes every other debt harder to pay.

A worked example: the car loan comes last

Here's a household with $200 a month in extra money, run through the calculator:

DebtBalanceAPRMinimumPaid off
Store card$1,80026%$55Month 8
Visa$5,20021%$130Month 23
Car loan$14,5007%$340Month 35

Here the smallest balance is also the highest rate, so snowball and avalanche give the exact same plan: debt-free in month 35 with about $3,392 in total interest. With minimum payments only, the same debts take 56 months and about $7,111 in interest.

Watch the car payment grow. In month 1 the car loan gets only its $340 minimum. After the store card and the Visa are gone, their minimums plus the $200 extra roll onto the car. By month 24 the car loan is getting $725 a month (its $340 plus $55, $130 and $200), and its balance falls fast.

When the car loan is almost paid off

Now say the car loan only has $1,500 left, but still has a $340 monthly payment. The other two debts stay the same.

  • Snowball targets the car loan first because it's the smallest balance. It's gone in month 3, and its $340 payment joins the extra money. Everything is paid off in month 14, with about $1,099 in interest.
  • Avalanche targets the 26% store card first. The car loan finishes on its own in month 5. Everything is still paid off in month 14, with about $1,105 in interest.

The two plans end in the same month and differ by about $6. But the snowball frees up a $340 monthly payment two months sooner. If your budget is tight, that freed-up cash is real breathing room.

Things to check before you add extra money to a car loan

  • Prepayment terms. Read your loan contract or ask the lender whether extra payments go to principal and whether there's any prepayment fee. The CFPB's auto loan pages explain what to look for.
  • Higher-rate debts first. Extra money on a 7% car loan usually saves less than the same money on a 20%+ credit card. That's why the car usually comes last.
  • Upside-down loans. If you owe more than the car is worth, paying it down faster can matter more if you're likely to sell or trade it in soon.
