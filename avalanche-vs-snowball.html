Debt Snowball vs Avalanche: Which Should You Use?

Same goal, two different orders. Here's how each method works, the actual trade-off between them, and how to test both on your own numbers.

Both the debt snowball and debt avalanche methods do the same three things every month: pay the minimum on every debt, then throw every extra dollar at one debt until it's gone, then roll that payment into the next one. The only difference is which debt goes first.

The core difference

Debt SnowballDebt Avalanche
Pays off firstSmallest balanceHighest interest rate
Optimizes forEarly wins, momentumLowest total interest
Best forStaying motivatedMinimizing cost

Debt avalanche method explained

The avalanche method sorts your debts by interest rate, highest to lowest, and pays extra toward the highest-rate debt first — no matter how large or small its balance is. Because it eliminates the most expensive debt first, less of your money goes to interest over the life of the payoff plan. It's the mathematically optimal order if your only goal is to pay the least amount of total interest.

Debt snowball method explained

The snowball method ignores interest rate and instead sorts by balance, smallest to largest. You pay off the smallest debt first, which is often faster to eliminate, and get the psychological win of closing an account and seeing your debt count go down. That payment then rolls into the next-smallest balance, and so on.

Which saves more money?

In most cases, avalanche saves more in total interest, because it targets the most expensive debt first. How much more depends entirely on your specific debts:

  • If your interest rates are all similar, the two methods land close to the same total cost and payoff date — the order barely matters.
  • If one debt has a much higher rate than the rest (a high-APR credit card next to a low-rate auto loan or student loan, for example), avalanche can save meaningfully more.
  • If your smallest-balance debt also happens to carry the highest rate, the two methods are identical — there's no trade-off to make.

Because this depends on your exact numbers, guessing isn't useful — running both scenarios is. The calculator's comparison table shows snowball, avalanche, and anything in between side by side: debt-free date, total interest, and total paid for each.

What about a blend?

Some people don't want to choose purely one factor. A blended strategy ranks each debt using a mix of its balance and its interest rate, rather than picking a single factor. Sliding toward snowball weighs balance more heavily; sliding toward avalanche weighs rate more heavily. It's a way to get some of the early-win momentum of the snowball method without fully giving up the interest savings of the avalanche method. The calculator's strategy slider does exactly this, recalculating the payoff order every month as balances change.

How to decide

  • Pick avalanche if you're disciplined about sticking to a plan regardless of visible progress, and your priority is minimizing what you pay in interest.
  • Pick snowball if you've struggled to stay motivated on a debt payoff plan before, and the boost of closing an account outweighs a modest difference in interest.
  • Pick a blend if you want a middle ground — enter your debts once and drag the slider to see how the trade-off actually looks for your situation.

Frequently asked questions

Which pays off debt faster, snowball or avalanche?

Avalanche is usually slightly faster and cheaper overall, since it targets the highest interest rate first. If your smallest debt also carries the highest rate, the two methods are the same.

Which method saves more money?

Avalanche almost always saves more in total interest. The size of the gap depends on how much interest rates vary across your debts — run your own numbers to see how big a difference it makes for you.

Can I use both methods at once?

Yes — a blended strategy that weighs both balance and interest rate is available via the strategy slider in the calculator, letting you find a middle ground between the two.
