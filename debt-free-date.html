How Your Debt-Free Date Is Calculated

There's no formula that spits out a single date — it's a month-by-month simulation. Here's exactly what it does.

A debt-free date isn't looked up from a table or computed with a single formula — with more than one debt, and money moving between them as each one is paid off, the only reliable way to get an accurate date is to simulate the payoff month by month. That's what the calculator does behind the scenes.

The month-by-month loop

For every month, in order, the calculator:

  1. Adds interest to every debt's balance, based on its own interest rate.
  2. Applies the minimum payment to every debt.
  3. Ranks the remaining debts by your chosen strategy — smallest balance first (snowball), highest rate first (avalanche), or a blend of both.
  4. Applies your extra payment to the top-ranked debt, and rolls over to the next one if that debt gets paid off with money left over.
  5. Checks which debts hit zero this month, and if one did, adds its old minimum payment to next month's pool of extra money — this is the "snowball" or "avalanche" effect building over time.

This repeats until every debt reaches zero. The month that happens is converted into a calendar date by counting forward from today — that's your debt-free date.

What moves the date

  • Extra payment amount — the single biggest lever. Even a modest increase compounds, because it snowballs into every subsequent debt once earlier ones are cleared.
  • Strategy (snowball vs. avalanche vs. blend) — changes which debt the extra payment attacks first, which can shift the date by months when interest rates vary a lot between debts. See snowball vs avalanche.
  • Interest rates — higher rates mean more of each payment is consumed by interest before it touches principal, which is why the minimum payment trap matters.
  • New charges or a lump sum — the calculator's advanced options let you model a one-time payment (a bonus or tax refund, say) or ongoing new charges on a card, and see exactly how each shifts the date.

Why two people with the same total debt can have different dates

Total balance alone doesn't determine a payoff date. Someone with $20,000 spread across two low-rate loans can be debt-free well before someone with $15,000 concentrated on one high-rate card, because interest is compounding on a different base every month. The order debts get paid off in, and how quickly freed-up minimum payments get redirected, both change the outcome — which is exactly why a month-by-month simulation is necessary instead of a shortcut formula.
