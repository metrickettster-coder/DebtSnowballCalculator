About This Calculator

Why it exists, how it works, and what it isn't.

What this is

A free tool for planning how to pay off multiple debts. Enter your balances, interest rates, and minimum payments, and it shows you a month-by-month plan comparing the debt snowball method (smallest balance first), the debt avalanche method (highest interest rate first), and anything in between.

Why it exists

Most debt payoff calculators make you pick one strategy up front and commit to it, or only handle one debt at a time. This one runs the full simulation for every strategy at once so you can see the actual trade-off — in debt-free date and total interest — for your specific numbers, instead of relying on generic advice about which method is "better."

How it works

Everything runs as plain JavaScript in your browser. There's no backend, no account, and no database — the calculation you see is happening on your device, not on a server somewhere. The full source is visible in your browser's developer tools, and the project's source repository is public (see Contact). See the privacy policy for exactly what is and isn't stored. For the exact math, see how your debt-free date is calculated.

What this isn't

This is a calculator, not financial advice. It projects outcomes based on the numbers you enter and the assumptions built into the simulation (fixed rates, consistent payments); it doesn't know your full financial situation and can't account for rate changes, fees, or life events. See the Terms page for the full disclaimer.

Who's behind it

PayoffSnowball is written and maintained by M.T., a procurement and program management professional with more than 15 years of experience managing budgets, supplier contracts, and cost control for large engineering programs. Day to day, that work means modeling payment schedules, comparing the total cost of competing options, and finding where small percentage differences turn into real money over time. This site applies the same discipline to household debt.

PayoffSnowball is independent. It isn't a bank, lender, credit counselor, or financial institution, it isn't affiliated with one, and it doesn't sell or recommend financial products. M.T. isn't a licensed financial advisor, and nothing here is personalized financial advice.

How we research and write

  • Numbers come from the calculator itself. Every worked example and study on this site is run through the same payoff engine you use on the calculator page, so you can reproduce it by typing in the same numbers. Our 10,000-plan snowball vs avalanche study is the largest example.
  • Facts are checked against primary sources. Rules about interest, balance transfers, and student loans are checked against government sources such as the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, the Federal Trade Commission, and Federal Student Aid. Each guide lists its sources at the bottom.
  • We say what we don't know. The calculator assumes fixed rates and steady payments, and every guide is written with that limit in mind. Where real life is messier (variable APRs, promotional rates, income-driven student loan plans), we say so.
  • Pages show their dates. Each guide shows when it was published and last reviewed. We revisit guides when the underlying rules change.
  • No fake trust signals. You won't find made-up reviews, testimonials, star ratings, or user counts here.
  • Corrections are welcome. If you spot an error, please tell us through the contact page. We'll fix it and update the page's date.

How the site is funded

The calculator and guides are free. To cover hosting and development costs, the site uses Google AdSense to show ads. Ads never change what the calculator computes or what the guides recommend, and advertisers don't see the numbers you type into the calculator. The privacy policy explains exactly what the ad service can and can't see, and how to manage ad personalization.
